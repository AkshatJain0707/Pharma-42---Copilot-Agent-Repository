# Research notes (verified in session, 20 Oct 2026). Cite source and date. Paraphrase.
## PoS benchmarks
- BIO/Informa Pharma Intelligence/QLS Advisors, "Clinical Development Success Rates and Contributing Factors 2011-2020" (published 17 Feb 2021). 9,704 programs, 12,728 phase transitions (cphi-online summary 2023; go.bio.org; cn-healthcare excerpt of report bullets).
- LOA from Phase I all modalities: 7.9% (2011-2020) vs 9.6% (2006-2015). Phase II transition 28.9% = largest hurdle. Hematology LOA 23.9% (highest), Urology 3.6% (lowest) of 14 areas. Oncology LOA 5.3%; immuno-oncology 12.4%. Programs with patient preselection biomarkers LOA 15.9%. Avg 10.5 years Phase I to approval. Biologics LOA 9.1% vs NMEs 5.7%; vaccines 9.7% (cphi summary).
- 2006-2015 report: oncology LOA 5.6%; Phase I transition 63.2%, Phase III 58.1% (that earlier report only).
- Newer: Citeline/Norstella 2024 article "Why Are Clinical Development Success Rates Falling?" exists (need fetch).
- Rare disease LOA higher than overall; chronic high-prevalence lower (go.bio.org).
- Citeline (Biomedtracker) 2014-2023 (published Apr 2024, In Vivo 16 May 2024): overall LOA from Phase I 6.7% (7.9% three years before). Phase I 47%, Phase II 28%, Phase III 55%. Phase I success >75% in 2006-08 fell below 40% in latest cohort. Declines attributed to biological complexity, competitive intensity, crowded pipelines. Rates vary widely by therapy area.
- Methodology matters: BIO 7.9% vs IQVIA 13.2% LOA for all therapies in a Nature Reviews Drug Discovery comparison (2025 article d41573-025-00036-8, dCGT focus); orphan gene therapies 27.6%. MIT (Wong, Siah, Lo, Biostatistics 2019) reported higher PoS than earlier estimates using different algorithms. => Always state dataset, period, definition of success, and how Phase I/II hybrids are handled.
## FDA CRL facts (secondary aggregators; primary = openFDA CRL database; VERIFY)
- openFDA CRL repository: 200+ letters released 10 Jul 2025 (applications 2020-2024, later approved); 89 more on 4 Sep 2025 (unapproved); 459 records as of 13 Aug 2026 (446 typed CRL); publication paused Apr 2026, resumed 10 Jul 2026 (ph3.bio, 20 Aug 2026; secondary).
- ph3.bio: 62.6% (286 of 457) of published letters carry manufacturing/quality deficiency section. Earlier 74% figure = 150 of first 202 letters (keyword classification, all later approved).
- Johns Hopkins (Dilek et al., 2026, 43 novel therapeutics with CRLs later approved, 2020-2024): facilities 65%, CMC 51%, labeling 44%, efficacy 26%, safety 26%, PK/PD 14%; median delay CRL to approval 1.28 years.
- McCarthy & O'Boyle 2026 (via ph3.bio, secondary): among NDAs with CRL, 64% later approved; CMC-cited 75% vs safety-cited 44.4%; CMC most common primary category 45%; extra delay ~709 days vs first-cycle approval.
- RSM US (Feb 2026), first 200+ letters: mean CRL-to-approval >2.4-2.5 years, median 1.5 years; CMC-primary median >21 months; over half of facility deficiencies from inspections FDA could not complete in time.
- Implication: CRL cause matters. CMC/facility CRLs are recoverable but slow; efficacy/safety CRLs recover less often. Selection bias: first tranche only later-approved products.
## IRA Medicare negotiation (sources: CMS final guidance IPAY 2028 dated 30 Sep 2025; KFF key facts; Milliman Jan 2026; CMS fact sheets). VERIFY live.
- Cycle 1 (IPAY 2026): 10 Part D drugs, MFPs effective 1 Jan 2026. Cycle 2 (IPAY 2027): 15 Part D drugs, MFPs effective 1 Jan 2027 (CMS published negotiated prices Nov 2025). Cycle 3 (IPAY 2028): 15 drugs, first with Part B, selection list released 27 Jan 2026, plus 1 drug selected for renegotiation; MFPs effective 1 Jan 2028.
- Cycle 3 timeline: written agreements by 28 Feb 2026; CMS initial offer by 1 Jun 2026; final written offer no later than 30 Sep 2026; negotiation period ends 31 Oct 2026 (KFF/CMS guidance). Publication of MFPs follows by statutory date (check; expected end Nov 2026).
- Generic/biosimilar availability: if determination made 2 Nov 2026 to 31 Mar 2028, MFP applies in 2028 only and drug ceases to be selected on 1 Jan 2029 (CMS guidance).
- Orphan Drug Exclusion expanded by P.L. 119-21 (section 71203, 2025 reconciliation law); 7- or 11-year clock for drugs with initial orphan designation measured from approval of first non-orphan indication (Milliman). First year the expanded exclusion applies = IPAY 2028.
- "Biosimilar Delay" provision: CMS fact sheet says all 15 IPAY 2028 drugs would have been selected absent the Biosimilar Delay (as stated in fact sheet; a mechanism letting drugs with high likelihood of biosimilar entry be delayed).
- Earlier-cycle negotiated prices can serve as an input to initial offers for therapeutic alternatives in later cycles (CMS fact sheet Nov 2025).
- Renegotiation introduced from IPAY 2028.
## MFN / TrumpRx (secondary sources; White House fact sheets primary; VERIFY)
- EO 14297 "Delivering Most-Favored-Nation Prescription Drug Pricing to American Patients" signed 12 May 2025. First company deal: Pfizer, 30 Sep 2025. 17 manufacturers (86% of branded sales per Health Affairs) by Apr 2026 (Regeneron deal 23 Apr 2026). 9 more announced 31 Aug 2026 (Alcon, Astellas, BeOne, BridgeBio, CSL, Kyowa Kirin, Sun Pharma, Teva, UCB; Time 1 Sep 2026): total 26 manufacturers, ~89% of branded market per White House. White House fact sheet 18 Sep 2026: MFN pricing available to all 50 state Medicaid programs.
- Terms of agreements not public (NPR 17 Sep 2026; Patients for Affordable Drugs); voluntary, not statutory, could be unwound (MedicalDaily). Commitments reportedly include MFN-aligned pricing for newly launched drugs (Duane Morris, 31 Aug 2026 alert, citing White House), Medicaid MFN pricing, direct-to-consumer cash prices via TrumpRx.gov (launched Feb 2026), US manufacturing investment pledges.
- JAMA research letter (Jul 2026) estimated ~USD 8.6bn/yr Medicaid savings from first 17 companies' MFN policies (via Time). Health Affairs: agreements unlikely to change near-term prices for most patients.
- Companies suggest Medicaid MFN deals will not lower Medicare prices (Time). Reference-pricing research (Maini): firms delay launches in lower-income countries to avoid price referencing.
- A CMS proposed rule on MFN pricing in Medicare Part B was reported at OMB (Health Affairs timeline; year/status to verify).
=> Net price forecasting must treat MFN as a launch-price and ex-US sequencing risk, Medicaid net price risk; non-public terms mean wide uncertainty.

- Generic price vs brand price before generic competition (AMP-based; invoice-based in brackets): 1 generic producer -39% (-31%); 2 producers -54% (-44%); 4 producers -79% (-73%); 6+ producers >-95% (both). Median generic-to-brand price ratio across sample ~40% (AMP). Measures generic PRICE vs pre-entry brand, not brand volume loss.
## Biosimilar uptake benchmarks (US; secondary sources, company-reported market reports; VERIFY)
- Adalimumab (Humira): 9 biosimilars entered 2023; <2% of adalimumab prescriptions in first year (Medscape, citing market data). CVS Caremark formulary change Apr 2024 (excluded reference product, preferred 2 private-label biosimilars + 1 branded) lifted biosimilar share to ~23% by Nov 2024 (Samsung Bioepis via Medscape). Uptake did not improve much after the spike through Feb 2025 (<20% non-Humira; private labels ~2/3 of biosimilar share) (J Manag Care Spec Pharm 2025; PMC12288720). Reference product held 73% share as of May 2025 (PharmaFocus America, IQVIA-sourced, secondary). Samsung Bioepis Q2 2026 report (27 Apr 2026): adalimumab biosimilar share 60% as of Feb 2026, includes Quallent private-label sales from 2026; shares exclude Cordavis private label (company-reported).
- Ustekinumab (Stelara): biosimilar share 27% at Q4 2025, 42% at Q1 2026 (Samsung Bioepis Q2 and Q3 2026 reports; company-reported); pace faster than adalimumab; private-label biosimilars launched alongside; ustekinumab biosimilar average ASP down 73% quarter on quarter by Q2 2026 (Samsung Bioepis). IQVIA (ACCESS! 2026): ustekinumab erosion pace significantly faster than adalimumab's first year; erosion curves steepening for newer launches; uneven across products.
- Mechanism: mass transition happened when originator removed from formularies / PBM private label (IQVIA via PharmaFocus). 
- FDA approved 96 biosimilars across 21 molecules, 71 launched, as of June 2026 (Samsung Bioepis Q3 2026 report, 23 Jul 2026).
- Eylea (aflibercept) biosimilar: Alvotech/Teva settlement with Regeneron grants US license entry date in Q4 2026 or earlier under certain circumstances (Teva 10-Q FY2026, citing 19 Dec 2025 announcement).
=> Erosion is driven by payer channel decisions (formulary exclusion, private label), not just entry. Use scenario on payer behavior.
## EU pharma package (secondary: Arthur Cox 10 Mar 2026; HSF Kramer Apr 2026; CMS Law 7 Jan 2026; Pharma Focus Europe 8 Apr 2026). VERIFY adoption/OJ status and application dates.
- Political agreement 11 Dec 2025; Council published compromise texts 6 Mar 2026 (Directive replacing 2001/83/EC; Regulation replacing 726/2004). Formal adoption: Arthur Cox indicated plenary ~15 Jun 2026; Pharma Focus (8 Apr 2026) said final adoption slated autumn 2026 then OJ publication. Status at 20 Oct 2026 NOT verified.
- Baseline changes from 8+2(+1) to 8 years regulatory data protection + 1 year market protection, with possible +1 (and +1) extensions; combined cap 11 years for standard products (Arthur Cox; CMS Law). Orphan/other periods differ. Transferable exclusivity voucher for antimicrobials (12 months, within data protection, once). 4 years data protection for repurposed products. Expanded Bolar exemption (allows pre-launch preparation so launch at day one of expiry). Applications for generics/biosimilars may be submitted in final two years of exclusivity (Pharma Focus).
- Directive transposition clock starts after OJ publication; legislation in force 20 days after publication (Arthur Cox).
=> Existing law (8+2+1) remains until new rules apply. EU LOE forecasts must state which regime applies to the product's MA date.
- UPDATE (CMS Law legal update, after 28 Sep 2026): Council formally adopted first-reading position 28 Sep 2026 after legal-linguistic revision; Parliament still must consider (early second reading agreement); plenary vote indicative Nov 2026, date not confirmed; NOT yet law. Directive Articles 83-84: 8 yrs RDP + 1 yr market protection, conditional extensions, cap 11 years (12 with transferable exclusivity voucher; Reg Arts 41-44). Entry into force 20 days after OJ publication; Directive transposition generally after 24 months (Art 222); full applicability expected by 2028 (Setterwalls, May 2026). Existing 8+2(+1) remains until then.
## FDA review clocks (FDA PDUFA VII commitment letter fda.gov/media/151712; PDUFA VIII letter fda.gov/media/193977 covers 1 Oct 2027 to 30 Sep 2032 with same cohort goals)
- NME NDAs and original BLAs: 90% acted on within 10 months of the 60-day filing date (standard) or 6 months of the 60-day filing date (priority) => about 12 and 8 months from submission. Non-NME NDAs: 10 months / 6 months from receipt. Class I resubmission 2 months, Class II resubmission 6 months from receipt. Major amendment in review cycle may extend goal date by 3 months (manufacturing supplement 2 months per letter text). PDUFA VII = FY2023-2027. FDA to communicate review issues ~8 weeks (standard) / 6 weeks (priority) before goal date (briefing memo). Goal = 90% of applications, not guarantee of action date.
- Worked date check: submission 2 Jan 2027 -> 60-day filing date 3 Mar 2027 -> standard goal ~3 Jan 2028; priority ~3 Sep 2027.
- FY2021 example: 52 of 60 NME/original BLA first-cycle approvals (Marks testimony Apr 2022) = 86.7% first-cycle approvals for applications acted on that year (a different measure than PoS from Phase I).
